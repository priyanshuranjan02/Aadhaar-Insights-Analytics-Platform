Project Overview

One dataset, nine dashboards, four models.

Aadhaar Insights Analytics Platform is an end-to-end analytics solution that transforms large-scale Aadhaar enrolment, demographic-update and biometric-update datasets into meaningful insight through advanced analytics and interactive visualization — built for researchers, policymakers, analysts and decision-makers.

Baseline, Linear Regression, Random Forest and XGBoost models forecast enrolment and update volumes.

Problem

Public data, private insight.

India's Aadhaar enrolment and update datasets are published at massive scale, but raw records rarely reach the people who could act on them — the data stays hard to query, harder to trend, and nearly impossible to forecast without dedicated tooling.

Motivation

Analytics for public-sector data.

Public datasets are one of the richest, most under-used sources for real-world data science. This project was built to show that the same techniques used in enterprise analytics — dashboards, ML forecasting, anomaly detection — apply just as well to civic and public-sector data.

Objectives

Explain, forecast, recommend.

Clean and structure raw Aadhaar data, surface trend and regional insight through interactive dashboards, forecast future enrolment with ML, automatically flag anomalies, and generate data-driven recommendations — end to end, deployed and usable.

Dataset

Three datasets, one public source.

The platform works with India's publicly released Aadhaar datasets covering enrolment activity, demographic updates and biometric updates. These records are aggregated at state and district level, giving the platform the granularity it needs for regional comparison, trend analysis and forecasting.

Enrolment Data

New Aadhaar enrolment volumes over time, by state and district.

Demographic Updates

Biometric Updates

Fingerprint, iris and photograph update activity.

Regional Aggregates

State and district-level rollups used for ranking and comparison.

Machine Learning

Four models, evaluated head to head.

Forecasting is implemented for Aadhaar enrolment, demographic updates and biometric updates, with four models evaluated against each other.

Baseline Model

Why selected: establishes a simple reference point — naive or historical-average forecasts — so every other model's improvement can be measured against something.

Advantages: trivial to compute, easy to explain, immediately available.

Limitations: ignores seasonality, regional variation and non-linear growth patterns in the data.

Linear Regression

Why selected: a fast, interpretable first real model — useful for confirming whether the relationship between time/features and enrolment volume is roughly linear.

Advantages: highly interpretable coefficients, fast to train, low risk of overfitting on limited data.

Limitations: struggles with the non-linear, regionally uneven growth patterns actually present in Aadhaar data.

Random Forest Regressor

Why selected: captures non-linear relationships and interactions between engineered features without heavy tuning.

Advantages: robust to outliers, handles non-linear patterns well, provides feature-importance for explainability.

Limitations: larger model size, less interpretable than linear regression, can still miss sharp trend shifts.

XGBoost Regressor

Why selected: gradient boosting typically gives the strongest predictive accuracy on structured, tabular data like this.

Advantages: high accuracy, handles complex interactions, regularization reduces overfitting.

Limitations: more hyperparameters to tune, slower to train than linear baselines, harder to explain to non-technical stakeholders.
